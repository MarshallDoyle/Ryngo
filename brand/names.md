# codegraph — Product Name Candidates

Working placeholder: `codegraph`. Looking for a single coined word or evocative real word that scans as a precise developer tool: PR-diffed graph IR, typed edges, pure-vs-effectful coloring, no LLM, no hosted backend.

Constraints applied: 1–3 syllables, globally pronounceable, evokes mapping / lensing / drilling-in / x-ray. Avoiding `graph`, `code`, `ast`, `lens`. Collisions sanity-checked via web search; obvious owners flagged.

## Top 5 Ranking

### #1 — **Plumb**
The strongest single word in the set. Three resonances stack: *plumb-line* (precision, true-vertical — fits deterministic, no-LLM), *plumbing* (the pipes/dataflow between modules — fits typed edges and pure-vs-effectful coloring), and *plumb the depths* (drilling-in, x-ray vision — the actual user job). One syllable, globally pronounceable, no ambiguous spelling. The verb form *"plumb your codebase"* lands without explanation, and it slots cleanly into CLI sentences (`plumb diff`, `plumb graph`, `plumb why`). Collision risk is real but moderate — `plumb.dev` is a small low-code workflow site, and there's a Python `plumb` lib, but no static-analysis tool owns this name. **This is the recommendation.**

### #2 — **Sextant**
Best metaphor on the list. A sextant *fixes your position by measuring angles between known points* — that is almost literally what a typed graph IR does. Two syllables, easy to say, rare enough that the .com may even be reachable. Mild risk: it sounds slightly antique, which can read as "thoughtful tool" (good for the Sourcegraph/Linear-adjacent crowd) or "trying too hard" (bad for the Unsloth-adjacent crowd). The precision angle pairs better with the spec than any other name.

### #3 — **Querra**
The strongest pure-coined option. Reads like a real word in English/Spanish/Italian, implies *query* without saying it, and is almost certainly clear of dev-tool collisions. Two syllables, unambiguous spelling, owns the distinct sound space. Risk: coined names need more brand investment to land — they don't carry meaning for free the way Plumb and Sextant do. Strong long-term play if you're willing to put marketing wattage behind it.

### #4 — **Strath**
Underrated. One syllable, evocative geography ("the floor of a valley"), and almost no collision in dev tooling. Reads as serious and a little Northern-European (which is on-brand for precision tooling). Slight risk that non-native English speakers may pronounce it as "strath" with a vowel that drifts; *Strathclyde*-adjacency is the main reference point. Good defensive #4 because the brand can grow into it.

### #5 — **Cartrix**
The most descriptive of the survivors — *cartography engine* unpacks immediately. The `-trix` suffix (matrix, helix) reads as technical and is a modern naming pattern. Memorable because it's pronounceable on first read. Risk: feels slightly like a SaaS-ops tool name (compare Cloudflare Cartograph), and it doesn't have the depth of Plumb's triple meaning. Solid utility pick.

---

## #1 Recommendation: **Plumb**

Pick this one.

Why:
- **One syllable.** Cannot be shortened, rolls off the tongue, fits in a CLI prompt without crowding.
- **Three meanings stack** onto your differentiators. Plumb-line → deterministic and exact (no-LLM). Plumbing → typed edges and pure-vs-effectful pipes. Plumb the depths → x-ray vision into code.
- **Verbs and noun phrases work.** `plumb the changes in this PR`, `plumb diff`, `plumb why`, `the plumb graph`. Beacon, Vista, Strata can't do this.
- **Tagline writes itself.** *"Plumb — the depths of your codebase, on a line."* or even just *"Plumb your repo."*
- **Collisions are survivable.** The biggest one is the small `plumb.dev` low-code site; not a static-analysis tool, not a household dev name. Domain availability is the real risk (see `domains.md`).

Fallback if `plumb.*` is fully squatted: **Sextant** — keeps the precision metaphor, owns more domain space.
